YouTube Thumbnail Sizes & Resolutions: A 300-Video Study

By 11tik

Published 31 August 2026 · Updated 3 September 2026

Measured eight public YouTube thumbnail variants across 300 video IDs: availability, dimensions, and median bytes. Sample-only—not all of YouTube.

Quick answer

In this 300-video sample (fetched 31 August 2026), lower public variants (default, mqdefault, hqdefault) always returned valid images (300/300). Higher filenames were common but not universal: maxresdefault and hq720 each validated for 286/300 (95.33%), and maxresdefault-webp for 284/300 (94.67%). The most frequent decoded size among valid observations was 1280×720. A missing maxres file in this sample did not mean the video was unavailable—lower variants still worked for those IDs. These are sample-scoped findings, not rates for all of YouTube.

11tik’s extractor probes the same public CDN filenames in the browser and keeps only sizes that actually load—matching the study’s “validate before trusting a filename” conclusion. This study does not download video or audio.

Introduction

Public videos expose thumbnail stills on i.ytimg.com under predictable filenames such as default.jpg, mqdefault.jpg, and maxresdefault.jpg. Filename patterns are widely documented, but how often each variant returns a valid image is harder to infer from a single video.

In our 300-video sample, fetched on 31 August 2026, we recorded 2,400 variant observations and classified each response after decoding image bytes—not HTTP status alone. Every rate below shows a numerator, denominator, and percentage. These are sample-only findings.

Why this study exists

Developers, CMS authors, and SEO practitioners often assume that a high-resolution filename always exists, or that missing maxresdefault means a video is unavailable. Those assumptions are risky without measurement.

This page complements our conceptual thumbnail size and resolution guide with empirical availability data from a fixed sample. The guide explains sizes in plain language; this study reports what we observed when we probed eight variants per ID.

Methodology

We requested eight public variants per video ID on i.ytimg.com (JPEG) and vi_webp/ (WebP) with User-Agent 11tik-research-study/1.0. Concurrency stayed at four or fewer parallel requests, with a 250 ms delay, 10 s timeout, and two retries.

Each URL received HEAD/GET checks. Classifications required decodable image bytes and consistent MIME types. High-res filenames that decoded to 120×90 preview tiles would be classified as placeholders; none appeared in this sample.

Sample composition

The sample contains 300 unique public video IDs drawn from a curated seed catalog, explicit verified supplements, and public YouTube channel RSS feeds. It is not a random sample of all YouTube uploads.

Before finalizing the sample, we excluded 42 IDs from an earlier pass that showed oEmbed 404 and no valid thumbnail endpoints—likely removed videos. The 300 IDs in this study were verified live at expansion time.

maxresdefault findings

In our 300-video sample, maxresdefault was available for 286/300 videos (95.33%). 14 IDs had no valid maxresdefault image while lower variants still returned valid images in the same run.

That pattern means a missing maxres file is not the same as “video unavailable” in this sample—it only shows the CDN did not serve that filename at measurement time. For per-video troubleshooting, see our maxresdefault guide.

What this study does NOT prove

  • It does not estimate rates for all YouTube videos—only for this 300-ID sample.
  • It does not reveal YouTube’s internal transcoding rules or Studio processing timelines.
  • It does not prove that a missing maxresdefault means a video is private, deleted, or age-restricted.
  • It does not define official thumbnail specifications—observed dimensions are decode results from public CDN responses.
  • Byte-size differences between JPEG and WebP in this sample are not a universal compression guarantee.

Limitations

  • Sample size: n = 300 unique public video IDs.
  • Sampling method: Curated seeds, RSS channel feeds, and explicit supplements—not random selection.
  • Fetch date: 31 August 2026. CDN availability can change when videos are reprocessed or removed.
  • Dimension counts aggregate valid observations across variants; they are not per-video exclusive counts.
  • 59 missing observations across all variants (mostly high-tier filenames) remain in the raw dataset alongside 2,341 valid images.
  • Watch vs Shorts URL shape: Probes used video IDs only. i.ytimg.com/vi/ID/ paths do not change when the pasted link is a watch, Shorts, live, or youtu.be URL—see the Shorts thumbnail guide for URL parsing.

Study FAQ

Does every YouTube video have maxresdefault?

Not in this sample. maxresdefault validated for 286 of 300 IDs (95.33%). Fourteen IDs lacked a valid maxres image while lower variants still returned valid images.

What size showed up most often?

Among 2,341 valid-image observations, 1280×720 was the most frequent decoded dimension (853 observations), consistent with maxres/hq720-class files when those variants validate.

Is this study a download tool?

No. It reports measured public CDN availability for a fixed sample. To download a public still for one video, use the YouTube Thumbnail Extractor or the download guide.

Conclusion

In our 300-video sample, lower thumbnail variants were nearly always present, while maxresdefault validated for 286/300 IDs (95.33%). Fourteen IDs lacked maxres while lower variants still worked—evidence that filename availability and video availability are not the same thing.
